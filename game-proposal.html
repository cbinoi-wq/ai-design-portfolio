01

Introduce the game

The louder the world becomes, the more you can swallow.

In Swallow the Static, hallucination is the way stress can make ordinary things feel louder, larger, and impossible to ignore.

The player controls a small circular void in a dreamlike city. Moving across objects consumes them if they are small enough. Each object makes the void grow, but it also causes the city to duplicate, tilt, and invent new objects.

The main interaction stays simple across three stages: move, swallow, grow. The emotional goal is not destruction for its own sake. It is the satisfying feeling of clearing a crowded space until only one calm memory remains.

01Release

Small objects disappear with soft, satisfying feedback.

02Power

The growing hole can consume what blocked it before.

03Doubt

Copied objects and false shadows make reality unreliable.

04Quiet

The player chooses what to keep instead of eating everything.

02

Case study · Browser arcade game

Hole.io

Created and published by Voodoo · Original release 2018 · Browser version on Poki

SWALLOW · GROW · REPEAT

I chose Hole.io because it is simple, playful, and surprisingly calming when I feel stressed or angry.

“It helps when I want to break everything. I can swallow everything, but inside a game.”

The player steers a hole through a bright city, beginning with tiny objects and growing large enough to swallow cars and buildings. Voodoo describes the game as satisfying and relaxing, while its design team focuses closely on suction, movement, friction, bounce, and camera easing. My experience helped me notice that destruction can feel like clearing clutter rather than acting violently.

Action analysis

Six events connect movement, feedback, and emotion.

The core action never changes, but the objects that can be swallowed expand. That simple progression creates a strong “one more object” rhythm.

Player actionGame responsePlayer understanding
Steer under a tiny object.The object drops, disappears, and adds growth.Anything smaller than the hole can be eaten.
Move toward something too large.It stays in place and blocks the route.Size is both the rule and the goal.
Circle through a cluster of small objects.Several quick drops create a smooth chain.Choosing a route makes growth faster and more satisfying.
Return to a car after growing.The car now bends and falls into the hole.Growth changes which parts of the world are possible.
Approach a larger rival hole.The larger hole becomes a threat.Scale creates risk as well as power.
Swallow a building.The city clears with large motion and impact feedback.The original obstacle has become a cathartic release.

What I learned

A satisfying interaction can communicate more than a complicated story. Hole.io uses scale, motion, and sound to turn one action into a complete loop. Swallow the Static keeps that clarity, but removes the leaderboard and uses growth to move from stress to quiet.

03

Critical discussion with AI

AI saw competition. I felt calm.

Verified research

What the sources support

  • Voodoo is the credited developer and publisher of Hole.io.
  • The core loop is steering a hole, swallowing smaller objects, and growing large enough to consume buildings.
  • Voodoo’s store text calls the experience simple, satisfying, relaxing, and suitable for short breaks.
  • Voodoo’s design listing names friction, bounce, suction force, camera motion, and easing as important parts of the game feel.
  • The current game supports both single-player and competitive multiplayer modes.

Two gaps I identified

What the first AI reading missed

  1. Destruction is not always experienced as aggression. AI described a power fantasy, but I experience the disappearing clutter as emotional release. Voodoo’s own description also calls the game oddly relaxing.
  2. Growth is not satisfying only because of points. The physical timing—the wobble, drop, suction, and sound—makes each object feel good to consume even before I check a score.
AI · First reading

“Hole.io is mainly a competitive power fantasy: the player destroys the city to become larger than opponents.”

My challenge

“That misses why I play. When I am stressed or angry, swallowing everything calms me down. Does the motion and disappearance make destruction feel more like clearing clutter?”

AI · Revised reading

“Yes. Competition explains the goal, but not the whole sensation. Smooth steering, visible growth, repeated suction, and an increasingly empty space can create order and release. The same mechanic can feel destructive and soothing at once.”

My conclusion

The pleasure comes from making visual noise disappear.

AI helped me separate the game’s competitive goal from my own emotional experience. The contradiction—destruction that feels calming—is more interesting than simply copying the original score system.

Design decision: my game will have no rivals, combat, or public leaderboard. The hole grows through soft, responsive motion while the world becomes quieter. The final stage asks the player to keep one memory instead of swallowing absolutely everything.

04

Narrative and concept

Hallucination as stress made visible.

My interpretation of Hallucination is a world where anxious thoughts become physical. A notification grows into a tower. One cup becomes five. Shadows detach from objects. The city is not objectively real; it is how an overwhelmed mind experiences it.

World

A soft city built from everyday mental clutter.

Phones, clocks, unfinished notes, cups, cars, and buildings float on a calm grid. Stress duplicates them and bends the streets into impossible loops.

Player role

A small quiet space inside the noise.

The player is not a hero or monster. They control an empty circle that can make crowded thoughts disappear and create room to breathe.

Main interaction

Steer toward smaller objects to swallow them.

The cursor, finger, or arrow keys guide the void. Consumed objects add size. Hallucinated copies flicker and give no growth, teaching the player to notice what is solid.

Desired experience

Cathartic, strange, and gently reflective.

The first two stages should feel immediately satisfying. The last stage slows down and asks whether making space must mean erasing everything.

Short narrative premise

A city forms from everything you tried not to think about. Become the quiet space inside it. Swallow the static, notice what is false, and leave one thing worth remembering.

Framing note: this is an artistic game about a personal feeling. It is not presented as therapy or as a treatment for stress or anger.

Interface + motion

Every object should feel good to clear.

  • Small objects wobble before falling inward.
  • The hole expands with a soft elastic ease, never a sharp flash.
  • False objects use a transparent double-image and dissolve on contact.
  • The camera pulls back slightly as the hole grows.
  • Reduced-motion mode removes wobble and camera scaling.

Sound + silence

The mix becomes simpler as the city empties.

  • Small objects make light wooden pops.
  • Large objects add a low, rounded whoosh.
  • False objects make a dry airy whisper instead of an impact.
  • Each stage removes one layer of background noise.
  • A visible mute control is always available.

Original browser-synthesized sketch. Final interface sounds may use Kenney’s CC0 Interface Sounds with credit.

07

Two-week build plan

Build the satisfying loop first. Add hallucination second.

Week 02Playable prototype
  1. Build one 2D scene: create a responsive top-down city in HTML Canvas or positioned HTML elements.
  2. Build movement: support pointer, touch drag, and arrow keys.
  3. Build the core loop: detect object size, swallow valid objects, and grow the hole.
  4. Build the journey: opening, three short stages, protected memory, ending, and replay.
  5. Add basic feedback: wobble, drop, size indicator, noise meter, and simple generated sound.

Week 2 output: a complete 3–5 minute game with temporary shapes and all stages working on mouse and touch.

Week 03Test, refine, publish
  1. Observe three players: record where they understand the size rule and where they get stuck.
  2. Test the feeling: ask whether swallowing feels satisfying, stressful, confusing, or repetitive.
  3. Refine pacing: tune object sizes, growth speed, stage length, and false-copy frequency.
  4. Polish access: readable contrast, keyboard control, mute, reduced motion, and phone layout.
  5. Publish and verify: test the direct URL, replay, interaction, and sound in a private browser window.

Week 3 output: a tested, refined game published as a new page inside this portfolio.

Scope lock

One 2D city. One steer-and-swallow interaction. Three short stages with 12–15 objects each. One protected memory and one ending. No 3D engine, multiplayer, enemies, account, shop, or online leaderboard.
